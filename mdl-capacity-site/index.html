Curvature-Weighted Capacity Allocation for Layer-Adaptive LLM Optimization

A Minimum Description Length (MDL) framework that allocates and prunes capacity across layers using curvature-adjusted gains, with closed-form solutions and efficient bisection-based optimization.

Abstract

Layer-wise capacity in large language models is highly non-uniform: some layers contribute disproportionately to loss reduction while others are near-redundant. Existing methods for exploiting this non-uniformity provide sensitivity estimates but no principled mechanism for allocation or pruning under hardware constraints. We address this gap with a unified, curvature-aware MDL framework. Our central quantity is the curvature-adjusted layer gain \(\zeta_k^2 = g_k^\top \widetilde{H}_{kk}^{-1} g_k\), which equals twice the maximal second-order reduction in empirical risk achievable by updating layer \(k\) alone. Normalized quality scores then drive two convex MDL programs for allocation and pruning. Both admit unique closed-form solutions parameterized by a single dual variable, computable in \(\mathcal{O}(K\log(1/\varepsilon))\) via bisection. We further prove an \(\mathcal{O}(\delta^2)\) transfer regret bound when curvature scores drift across domains.

Method at a Glance

  1. Estimate per-layer curvature-adjusted gain using gradient and regularized Hessian blocks.
  2. Normalize gains into quality scores \(q_k\).
  3. Solve allocation program to distribute added capacity under budget \(B\).
  4. Solve pruning program to meet sparsity target \(S\) while preserving high-gain layers.
  5. Transfer decisions across domains with bounded regret when scores drift.
